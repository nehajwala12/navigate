### Q1 expanded — nine markers that the timing is the seller's, not the market's

1. FY24 carries **+$69.1MM** of diligence adjustments; the most recent twelve months carry **−$9.5MM**. The apparent climb from $33MM to $174MM of standalone EBITDA is **adjustment asymmetry**, not four years of operating improvement. [VERIFIED — Vol 4 datacube rebuild]
2. **$9.3MM of unearned, execution-contingent savings** sit inside the 2025A "actual" — $4.2MM contingent on an ERP not yet built, $5.1MM on a Puritan's wind-down completing Q1-2027. [CIP p99]
3. **~$24MM of orders pulled forward** from 2026, disclosed in a footnote, left in the number. **No pro-forma line removes it.** At the 50.8% contribution rate, ~$12MM of margin sits in 2025 that belongs to 2026.
4. **December-2025 shipment-led load-in**: +$12.6MM YoY at Walmart/CVS/Amazon/Kroger while Walmart consumption was flat; CVS then shipped **$0.2MM and $1.0MM** in January and February 2026. [`1.3.39`, `1.5.24`]
5. A **two-year, $126.4MM audited working-capital liquidation** ($67.4MM FY24 + $58.9MM FY25) presented as "disciplined working capital management" — with a **further $10MM release underwritten for 2026E**. Finished goods fell 29.8%. [`1.3.17` p6/p8]
6. A **13.7-day DSO compression** (59.5 → 45.8 days) presented as a flat 48-day steady state. You can collect faster once. Expected credit losses rose **5x to 1.41%** of a shrinking book, driven — per the auditors' own note — by *"negotiations about variable consideration"*: trade-spend dispute with retailers.
7. **FY25 IT capex frozen at $2.2MM** against $23.3MM (FY23) and $14.9MM (FY24) — a pre-sale freeze on the systems estate the buyer inherits. [`1.11.6.1`]
8. The **co-manufacturing base was re-papered during the sale process** — seven agreements executed or amended Nov-2025 → May-2026, change-of-control terms unread. [`1.8.1.1`]
9. **The seller's own QoE grew between drafts.** Between the Volume 2 and Volume 4 vintages, FY24 EBITDA gained **+$4.4MM** via a $2.2MM increase to the least-supported adjustment in the set and a **new $2.49MM "audit adjustment"** absent from the earlier vintage entirely. Both moves run the seller's way, in the year that anchors the improvement narrative. [`1.3.6` vs `1.3.37` — VERIFIED]

**What is genuinely durable — stated fairly:** Walmart's Nature's Bounty growth is consumption-led (POS +101% vs shipments +62%, Jan–May 2026); Costco is +8.0% with zero adjustments in its history; and the category itself grows — **at the premium, science-backed end, which is not where these brands sit.** No perimeter brand holds more than 2.1% of the US vitamin market [EXTERNAL — Euromonitor via Reuters]. The book is re-freezing around **three retailers (68.9% of net sales)** and a handful of trend SKUs (magnesium glycinate, berberine) whose Walmart velocity **crested in January 2026 and has declined every four-week period since**. That is a concentration trade, not durability.

### Q2 expanded — the number the seller does not show

The seller's headline metric is **127.5% "FCF conversion."** Its own definition (CIP p94 n.8) — adjusted EBITDA − capex + the working-capital release — is struck **before cash interest, before cash taxes, before the $10MM Puritan's exit and before the $34MM standalone IT build**. It is not a credit metric, and it is excluded from this analysis by standing decision. The same business, measured the way a lender is repaid, produces **negative $36MM per year through separation** at the working frame (full bridge at §5).

### Q3 expanded — why recovery is fragile exactly when it is needed

Nature's Bounty **cannot be separated from the seven-site network** (43–66% of every factory, 36–50% of both pack sites), so recovery is modelled as **NB + the network as one unit**, with tail brands as **assignable contracts net of consent and transition cost** — not an asset sale (Nuun is 0% in-house; GARD <1%). IATA §2.14 prices consent failure against the buyer: pass-through benefit dies at the first contract expiration, and Nestlé does not remain secondarily liable. A distressed operator loses the line review; Costco's assortment already contracted from 60 to 43 active items even as sales rose; the tail brands need **18–29% of sales in marketing** to hold position — the first spend a distressed operator cuts. And **no IP schedules of any kind exist in the room**: registered marks, jurisdictions and encumbrances are unknown, on a credit whose recovery case is a brand.
